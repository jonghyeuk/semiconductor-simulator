# CLEANROOM INK — a visual philosophy

Ink on warm paper, one weight, no shading for prettiness. Faces become hoods,
goggles and masks — the anonymized cleanroom figure, where craft shows in the
stance of a body, not features. Space breathes through halftone screentone;
equipment is quoted as silhouette and cross-section, felt at the periphery so
the human moment holds the center. Color is rationed to a single spot and is
never decoration — it is information given a body: the one word on the HMI, the
alarm, the line of English a person must read to work safely. The reader is
trained, panel after panel, that the lit word is the one to learn. Composition
follows the grammar of comics — bold frame, decisive gutter, a balloon whose
tail points. Each panel is meticulously inked and painstakingly composed, a page
torn from a well-loved industrial manga that happens, quietly, to teach the exact
English met on a first shift.
